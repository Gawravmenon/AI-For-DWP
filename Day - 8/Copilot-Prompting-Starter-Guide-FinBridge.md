# Copilot Prompting Starter Guide (FinBridge)

## Who this is for
This guide is for non-technical office staff who want to get useful results from Microsoft 365 Copilot quickly.

The goal is simple: ask clearly, give context, and tell Copilot what a good answer looks like.

---

## The easiest way to write a good prompt
Use this 4-part formula:

**Task + Context + Output + Tone**

You can remember it as: **"What + Why + How + Style"**

- **Task (What):** What do you want Copilot to do?
- **Context (Why):** What is this about, and who is it for?
- **Output (How):** Format, length, and structure you need.
- **Tone (Style):** Friendly, formal, concise, etc.

### Quick template
"Please **[task]** using **[context/source]**. Give me **[what the answer should look like and how long it should be]** for **[audience]** in a **[tone]** tone."

### Example using the template
"Please draft a follow-up email using notes from today’s supplier meeting. Give me 5 bullet points and a short action list for the procurement team in a professional but friendly tone."

## Outlook prompt examples (real, ready to use)

### 1) Summarize a long email thread
"Summarize this email thread in 6 bullet points. Include: key decisions made, open questions, and who owns each next action. End with a one-line suggested reply I can send to confirm next steps."

### 2) Draft a clear follow-up email after a meeting
"Draft a follow-up email to the Finance Operations team based on today’s 10:00 AM meeting. Include: decisions agreed, actions with owners, and deadlines. Keep it under 180 words and use a professional, helpful tone."

### 3) Prioritize inbox messages
"Review my unread emails from today and group them into: Urgent, This Week, and FYI. For each urgent email, suggest a one-sentence reply I can send now."

Tip: If needed, add "ask me for missing details before drafting" to avoid rework.

---

## Word prompt examples (real, ready to use)

### 1) Turn rough notes into a polished document
"Use the notes in this document to create a one-page project update for senior managers. Include headings for Progress, Risks, and Next Steps. Keep language plain and avoid jargon."

### 2) Rewrite for clarity
"Rewrite this draft so it is easier for non-technical staff to read. Shorten long sentences, remove repetition, and keep the meaning the same. Highlight any sections that are still unclear."

### 3) Create a professional first draft from scratch
"Create a first draft of a client meeting agenda for a 45-minute quarterly service review. Include objectives, discussion points, decision items, and a 5-minute Q&A section. Use a formal business tone."

Tip: Ask Copilot for two versions, for example: "Give me a short version and a detailed version."

---

## Excel prompt examples (real, ready to use)

### 1) Explain what is happening in the data
"Analyze this table of monthly ticket volumes and resolution times. Identify trends over the last 6 months, any unusual spikes, and the top 3 likely causes. Summarize in plain English for a manager update."

### 2) Build formulas with explanation
"In column G, I want cells to show ‘At Risk’ if remaining support hours are below 4 and the status is not ‘Resolved’. Please write the exact formula and explain it in plain English."

### 3) Prepare a quick performance view
"Create a summary table by team showing: total tickets, average resolution time, and how many missed the SLA target. Then recommend the best chart and explain why in simple terms."

Tip: If the answer feels too general, add a little more detail, such as a date range, team name, or which columns to use.

## Make prompts better in 10 seconds
Before you send a prompt, quickly check:

1. Did I clearly say the task?
2. Did I give enough context (source, audience, time period)?
3. Did I request the output format I actually need?
4. Did I set a tone and length?

If not, add one missing detail and send again.

---

## Data-safety reminder (Day 1 habit)
From Day 1 of this programme, the key habit is still the same:

**Do not paste confidential client data into external AI tools.**

You can use Copilot confidently in approved FinBridge and Microsoft 365 tools. Just keep to normal data-handling habits: only share what is needed, and remove client names or personal details unless they are necessary for the task.

When in doubt, pause and check with your manager or the security team before sharing content.

## One-page prompt cheat sheet
Use this line when you are stuck:

"Copilot, help me **[task]** using **[source/context]**. Return **[format + length]** for **[audience]** in a **[tone]** tone, and include **[specific extras: actions, risks, deadlines, etc.]**."

The more specific you are, the more useful Copilot becomes.
